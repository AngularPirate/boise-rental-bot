Reading 54,000 AI outputs before the launch call

A new AI recap looked great in the demo. I read what it would actually produce for our real clients, then recommended a staged rollout behind an explicit quality gate instead of a full launch.

Context

The platform already ran an AI summarization pipeline in production. The next step on the roadmap I own was a new AI recap capability built on top of it.

I own the AI and coaching-intelligence track. That made the launch call mine to recommend.

Problem

Demos are picked from good inputs. Real clients aren't. Some log a lot, some barely log at all, and some have history that doesn't line up. A recap that reads well for the median client can be wrong, thin, or awkward for the long tail.

Coaching runs on trust. A confident, wrong recap in front of a client costs more than a missing one. So the question wasn't "does it work?" It was "is it good enough for every client we'd show it to, and what happens when it isn't?"

Decisions

Size it from production, not from the demo

I pulled what the capability would produce across the real client base: 54,000+ outputs from 3,400+ clients. I queried production myself and read through the results instead of a hand-picked sample.

Trade-off: slower than a demo review. In exchange, the long tail showed up before launch instead of after.

Write the gate down before judging

I defined what "good enough" meant as an explicit quality gate, so the launch decision rested on a written bar rather than on how the last few examples felt.

Trade-off: a strict gate holds back some outputs that would have been fine. I'd rather explain a held-back recap than a wrong one.

Stage the rollout instead of launching to everyone

I recommended starting where the outputs cleared the gate and widening only as quality held, rather than turning it on for every client at once.

Trade-off: slower to full reach. It keeps the decision reversible and gives us real usage to measure before each step.

Outcome

The recommendation was a staged rollout behind an explicit quality gate, not a full launch. This page covers that decision and how I got to it. Rollout results aren't included here.

The habit carried forward. When the model behind our production summaries was retired, I led the replacement and set the fallback plan with engineering, and the same question applied: is the new output good enough, and what happens when it isn't?

What I'd do next

Make the gate cheap to rerun. An automated scorer, checked against human reads, would let every model change or prompt change go through the same bar before it reaches a client. I'd also track what slips past the gate after launch, so the bar gets tuned on evidence.

I'm building the public version of this now: Gatekeeper, a quality gate for AI-drafted lifecycle messages on synthetic data.
