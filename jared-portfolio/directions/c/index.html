How I work

I don't hand a spec to a designer, then a designer to an engineer, then wait. On my team I'm the designer. I write the data model. I check the spec against production with SQL before anyone builds on it, and I open the pull request that engineers review and ship.

AI is in every one of those steps. I've turned the repeatable parts of my work into 35+ skills and agents, from interview prep to release notes, and written them down well enough that a teammate runs them without me. What stays human is the judgment: what to build, whether the math is right, and whether the output is good enough for a customer to see.

    Building in public

    Gatekeeper In progress

    A quality gate for AI-drafted lifecycle messages. Synthetic customers move through a made-up SaaS trial. A model drafts onboarding nudges and win-backs, a rubric scores each one on brand voice, grounding, personalization, compliance, and tone, and the gate routes it to auto-send, human review, or block. A staged-rollout view shows the call segment by segment.

    The demo runs on precomputed outputs, so there's no backend or API key. One question drives it: where should the human sit in the loop for AI-written messages, and how do you measure it?
