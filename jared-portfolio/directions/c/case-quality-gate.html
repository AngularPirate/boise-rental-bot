I read 54,000 AI outputs, then argued against a full launch

A new AI recap looked great in the demo. Before anyone turned it on, I read what it would really produce for our clients, and recommended a staged rollout behind an explicit quality gate.

  • WhereA B2B coaching platform, 3,600+ active clients
  • RolePM, AI and coaching-intelligence track
  • The read54,000+ outputs, 3,400+ clients

Context

The platform already ran an AI summarization pipeline in production. Next on the roadmap I own was a new AI recap capability built on top of it, and the launch call was mine to recommend.

Problem

Demos are picked from good inputs. Real clients aren't tidy. Some log a lot, some barely log at all, some have history that doesn't line up. A recap that reads well for the typical client can be wrong or thin for the long tail.

Coaching runs on trust. So the question wasn't whether it worked. It was whether it was good enough for every client we'd show it to, and what would happen when it wasn't.

A confident, wrong recap costs more than a missing one.

Decisions

Size it from production, not the demo

I pulled what the recap would produce across the real base, 54,000+ outputs from 3,400+ clients, querying production myself.

Trade-off Slower than a demo review. The long tail shows up before launch instead of after.

Write the bar down first

I defined "good enough" as an explicit quality gate, so the call rested on a written standard rather than on how the last few examples felt.

Trade-off A strict gate holds back some fine outputs. A held-back recap is easier to explain than a wrong one.

Stage it, don't flip it

Start where outputs cleared the gate. Widen only while quality holds.

Trade-off Slower to full reach, but reversible, and every step is measured on real usage.

Outcome

My recommendation was a staged rollout behind an explicit quality gate rather than a full launch. This story is about that decision. Rollout results aren't included here.

The same question came back when the model behind our production summaries was retired. I led the replacement and the fallback plan with engineering, and the bar didn't change: is the new output good enough, and what happens when it isn't?

What I'd do next

Make the gate cheap to rerun: an automated scorer, checked against human reads, so every model or prompt change clears the same bar before it reaches a client. Then track what slips through after launch and tune the bar on that evidence.

The public version is underway. Gatekeeper asks the same question of AI-drafted lifecycle messages, on synthetic data.
